About

Two systems. One engineer.

I'm an Automobile Engineering student at SRM Institute of Science and Technology, specializing in Automotive Electronics. I move between two worlds that are increasingly the same vehicle: the mechanical side — chassis, powertrains, wiring — and the electronics side — microcontrollers, sensors, and the buses that let them talk to each other.

As Team Principal at Team 1.618 (Team Golden Ratio) — after stints as Media Head and Powertrain Head — I work Formula Student builds from the chassis floor to the ECU. Outside the team, I'm usually deep in a personal build — most recently an RFID keyless ignition for a scooter, and a holographic music/weather display running on an ESP32.

RFID Keyless Ignition & Security System

Replaced the mechanical key of a 12V scooter with RFID authentication using an Arduino Uno and MFRC522 reader. Tag UIDs are verified over SPI against an authorized list stored in flash memory before the ignition is powered.

Period: Oct 2026 – Nov 2026
Stack: Arduino Uno, MFRC522, SPI
Protection: Fuse, reverse-polarity diode, capacitor, 12V→5V buck converter
Source: GitHub Repository ↗
Shipped

Holographic Music / Weather Display

An ESP32-WROOM-32 driving an SSD1306 OLED, synced live to Apple Music / Spotify through a Python relay script running on a Mac. Rendered as a faux-holographic HUD.

MCU: ESP32-WROOM-32
Display: SSD1306, 128×64, I2C
Source: Apple Music / Spotify via Python relay on Mac
Shipped

Quick Up-Shifter

Formula Student ignition-cut quick-shifter. Load cell input through an ADC and buck converter, switched via relay, tuned to reject false triggers under vibration.

Cut window: 0.65–0.85 ms
Stack: Arduino R3, Load Cell, ADC, Buck Converter, Relay
Result: Installed and raced on the Team 1.618 car
